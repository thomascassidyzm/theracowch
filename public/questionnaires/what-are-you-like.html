What are you like, anyway?

Thirty little moments everyone knows — and one question each time: which would you do first?

The party where you only know the host. The yoghurt past its date. The bulb that’s gone and the ladder that’s buried. You already know what you’d do — you do it every day. This just pays attention.

While you answer, nothing moves. No bars creeping, no score ticking — what you can’t watch, you can’t steer, and that’s what keeps the answers honest.

Then, at 10, 20 and 30 answers, the curtain lifts: six things about you come into focus — the familiar five, plus risk appetite, the one everybody argues about later. Sharper at every look.

Every result is relative: put 100 people in a room — you’d be standing around here. No types, no verdicts, no right answers.

And if none of the options is you — or the honest answer is “it depends” — every moment has a way to say exactly that. It’s a real answer, not a cop-out.

This isn’t a test and it isn’t a diagnosis — it’s a mirror, and a playful one. Everything you tap stays on your device. Bring anything that catches you to your chat with Mandy.

So — what are you like?

How to read this: put 100 people in a room. Each band below shows where you’d most likely be standing — and how sure we can honestly be. A wide band means we’d need more answers before narrowing it; that’s the truth, not a flaw.
A note on honesty: personality measures — all of them, including this one — explain only a modest slice of what a person actually does. Treat everything above as an indication worth exploring, never a label.
